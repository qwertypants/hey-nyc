# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. This is a small open-source project about a city, and it should feel
like one.

## What is expected

- Assume the person you are replying to has a reason you have not thought of.
  Most reports about bad data turn out to be a genuine upstream problem that
  nobody noticed yet.
- Be specific about the data. "This record is wrong" is much more useful than
  "this is broken", and it is the only kind of report the maintainer can act on.
- Accept that "no" can be the right answer, and that it is often a design
  decision rather than a judgement of your work.
- Credit other people's work. The data belongs to NYC DOT; the basemap data
  belongs to the OpenStreetMap community.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, personal attacks, or sustained disruption of discussion. This
  includes conduct outside the repository's issues and pull requests when it is
  directed at the project or at people in it.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing anyone's private information without explicit permission.
- Deliberately misrepresenting the data. Do not edit `public/data/` to make a
  point, and do not present a filtered or cherry-picked view of the dataset as
  what the project publishes.
- Adding a feature that invents data the source does not have — ratings, hours,
  prices, "best of" rankings — and presenting it as though it came from NYC Open
  Data. This project has one promise: every field is traceable to a real column
  in a real public dataset. Breaking it is worse than a missing feature.

## Scope

This applies in the repository — issues, pull requests, discussions, commits —
and to anyone representing the project in public.

## Reporting

Report a problem privately to the maintainers by opening a
[GitHub security advisory](https://github.com/eat-outside-nyc/eat-outside-nyc/security/advisories/new)
rather than a public issue. Reports about conduct will be handled confidentially,
reviewed, and acted on. Maintainers who do not follow this code are subject to
the same consequences as anyone else participating in the project.

## Enforcement

Maintainers will respond in proportion to the problem. In practice that usually
means a private word, a documented warning, or a temporary or permanent ban from
project spaces. The aim is to fix the behaviour, not to punish the person.
